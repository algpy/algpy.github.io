Research

Making visual models useful when the label set is incomplete.

My projects follow one technical question across three settings: novel class discovery, generalized category discovery, and on-the-fly category discovery.

Core question

How should a model learn, recognize, and create categories it was never given?

In novel class discovery, I transfer relations from known-class predictions to unlabeled categories. In generalized discovery, I move that transfer from logits to features. In online discovery, I make category birth an explicit statistical decision instead of a fallback heuristic.

Research trajectory

The same problem becomes more open as supervision is removed and data arrives over time.

  1. 2022 - 2023Novel class discovery

    Class-relation knowledge distillation

    Proposed a transfer strategy that preserves relations between labeled and novel classes with learnable per-sample weighting. The project produced the ICCV 2023 co-first-author paper and state-of-the-art results on nearly all evaluated datasets.

  2. 2023Generalized category discovery

    Adaptive knowledge transfer

    Extended the ICCV approach from logit transfer to feature representations so known and novel classes could be discovered together. This work won the OOD-CV SSB 2023 Generalized Category Discovery track on ImageNet-1k.

  3. 2025 - 2026On-the-fly discovery

    DP-BOA and Bayesian category birth

    Designed an online Dirichlet-process model that turns “assign or create” into a posterior-predictive decision for every incoming sample. The project was accepted to ECCV 2026 and reports state-of-the-art results on nearly all evaluated datasets.

01
ICCV 2023Co-first authorOct 2022 - Mar 2023

Class-relation Knowledge Distillation for Novel Class Discovery

Peiyan Gu, Chuyu Zhang, Ruijie Xu, and Xuming He

Novel class discovery learns unlabeled categories using supervision from labeled known classes. This paper represents each novel class by the predicted class distribution of a model trained on known categories, making relations between known and novel classes explicit.

Method

A class-relation knowledge-distillation objective preserves relation information during discovery. A learnable per-sample weighting function adapts transfer strength according to semantic similarity between known and novel classes.

Evaluation

Evaluated on CIFAR100, Stanford Cars, CUB, and FGVC-Aircraft; the method exceeded prior state of the art by a significant margin on almost all benchmarks.

02
ECCV 2026First author · AcceptedSep 2025 - Mar 2026

DP-BOA: Dirichlet-Process Birth-or-Assign for On-the-Fly Category Discovery

Peiyan Gu, Zixin Teng, and Xuming He

On-the-fly category discovery must decide, for every incoming sample, whether to assign it to an existing category or create a new one. DP-BOA replaces radius- and hash-based birth heuristics with an explicit posterior-predictive comparison.

Method

An online Dirichlet-process Gaussian mixture with a Normal-Inverse-Wishart prior. Labeled data calibrates a shared prior and warm-starts known-category posteriors; test samples are classified and category statistics are updated online.

Decision rule

For each sample, DP-BOA compares predictive evidence for every existing category with evidence for a new category induced by the Dirichlet-process prior.

Results

Across standard on-the-fly discovery benchmarks, the method consistently outperformed strong baselines, with particularly strong novel-class discovery while retaining competitive known-class accuracy.
